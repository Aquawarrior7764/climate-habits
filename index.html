Money doesn't grow on trees. Neither does meat or gasoline for cars and airplanes. Yet these are some of the biggest contributors of climate change caused by individual actions (Wynes and Nicholas). 

0.8 tons of greenhouse gases saved per person every year by eating a plant-based diet

2.4 tons of greenhouse gases by living car-free

4× MORE than recycling saves (compared with a plant-based diet)

The figures above are averages for developed countries from Wynes and Nicholas.

Although the causes of climate change can be discouraging because may people frequently eat meat and use cars and airplanes, these circumstances also offer some simple, effective habits to adopt in order to reduce climate change: eating no or less meat; flying less on airplanes; and not using, using cars less or using public transportation more. 

Food and eating habits

Why it matters

 Livestock production contributes to 14.5% of the total greenhouse gasses. Lowering the demand for livestock production is one of the most effective actions an individual can take to reduce climate change. 

Look at this poor cow! :(

CHANGES YOU CAN MAKE

  • Switch to a Vegan or Vegetarian diet/Eat less meatThis is the most effective way to reduce demand. And though not everyone can completely remove meat from their diet, cutting down on it still helps to reduce CO₂ emissions. (EMMA fact check?)
  • Eat fish or poultry instead of pork and beefMeat production is very inefficient, with meat requiring 11 times more fossil fuels compared to plant protein. Efficiency reduces in order of: fish, poultry, pork, and beef. Higher efficiency meats cut down on supply chain emissions and those created by the animal itself.
  • Buy your meat locally This can cut down on emissions produced by trucks and boats that bring meat from the mainland to Hawai’i 
  • Buy meat from farms that use non-fibrous, high-quality, animal feeds Using quality, fibrous animal feeds can result in increased greenhouse gasses caused by digestion as well as increased manure

Transportation habits

Why it matters

Living without a car saves about 2.4 tons of greenhouse gases per person every year. Wynes and Nicholas say that it's one of the 4 most effective ways to cut greenhouse gases for a person.

Not driving a car at all is not realistic for everyone, but partial changes will help too.

26–76%

lower emissions when a trip moves from a sedan to public transit, even over the same distance

Research by Chester et al. (2013), as reported in Wynes and Nicholas (2017).

CHANGES YOU CAN MAKE

  • Use the Bus and SkylineApps like Transit and Google or Apple maps show bus and rail times.
  • CarpoolingIf you and a friend are going the same way, carpool. It's a good way to socialize, too.
  • Walking/BikingConsider biking or walking when going short distances. It saves on gas AND is good exercise.
  • Do multiple errands at onceSave gas and the Earth by going somewhere when you’re already near, rather than making more trips than you need to.
  • Fly on planes lessPlanes are a big contributor to greenhouse gas emissions (Wynes and Nicholas), reduce demand for them by flying less often
